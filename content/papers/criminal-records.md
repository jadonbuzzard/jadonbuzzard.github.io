I study how employers interact with the workers with a criminal record and how those interactions change when employers are hit by adverse shocks. Using confidential person-level linkages between the Criminal Justice Administrative Records System (CJARS) and the Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics (LEHD) infrastructure, I construct a longitudinal employer-employee panel that follows workers with and without criminal records across establishments, industries, and regions from 2000 onward. I first test whether workers with records are disproportionately concentrated in low-wage, cyclically sensitive industries. Next, exploiting national variation in industry employment growth (e.g., during the Great Recession), I test whether firms hit by industry shocks reduce hiring and increase separations of workers with records relative to nonrecord holders, controlling for various worker and firm-level confounders. Finally, I identify mechanisms and whether these responses widen the conviction gap in employment, earnings, and reliance on safety-net programs.
