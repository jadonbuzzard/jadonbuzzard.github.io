Hundreds of local law enforcement agencies across the United States now use unmanned aerial vehicles equipped with audio, video, and thermal imaging technology to respond to emergency calls for service. These rapidly-proliferating "Drones as First Responder" (DFR) programs are predicted to substantially improve the quantity and quality of information available to police, but no comprehensive evaluation of DFR programs currently exists. I have collected detailed geolocational flight logs for the near-universe of publicly-documented drone flights (~300k) conducted by 200+ local law enforcement agencies to examine how drone flight characteristics vary across departments. For a subset of these departments, I pair this with granular data on calls-for-service, crime, and use-of-force to provide the first comprehensive study of how DFR programs impact modern police work.
